    What is rebalancing?

    You pick a target mix, such as 60% stocks, 30% bonds and 10% cash. Over time the asset that grows fastest takes up more of the portfolio, so your real mix drifts away from what you chose, usually toward more risk.

    Rebalancing means selling some of what grew and buying what lagged to get back to the target. Calendar rebalancing does this on a schedule, such as once a year. Threshold rebalancing waits until an asset drifts past a set band, such as 5 percentage points.

    Rebalancing is mainly about keeping your risk where you chose it, not about earning the most. In many scenarios the untouched portfolio ends up worth more simply because it drifted into more stocks, and the comparison table shows the bumpier ride that came with it.
